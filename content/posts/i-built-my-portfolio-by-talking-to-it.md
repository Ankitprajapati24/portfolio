Recruiters skim. A list of skills doesn't show how I think, what I care about, or what I sound like. So I decided the portfolio itself should be a project.

The twist: I barely typed any of it. I **spoke** it.

## Holding one key and talking

I used [Wispr Flow](https://wisprflow.ai) for dictation. Hold a key, talk, and the words appear wherever the cursor is. I'd describe what I wanted, and an AI coding tool would build it.

That sounds like a small change. It wasn't.

### 1. Speed

I can speak a full design brief in thirty seconds. Typing the same thing takes five minutes, and by then I've already cut half the detail. Because asking was so cheap, I iterated far more.

### 2. I could think in my own language

I naturally mix Hindi and English, and Flow kept up. At one point I literally said:

> pet hona chahiye, jo poore din, poori jagah ghoome, bachchon ki tarah harkatein kare

That became **Echo**, the little orange cat who wanders around the page, does zoomies and falls asleep. I would never have typed that sentence. I'd have written something stiff like "add an animated mascot", and got something stiff back.

### 3. Feelings, not specs

"When I hover on the name it should tilt like this website." That one line got me a better result than a paragraph of requirements would have. Saying what I want something to *feel like* turned out to be the best prompt.

### 4. I stayed in flow

My eyes stayed on the design instead of on the keyboard. That's the part that's hard to explain until you try it.

It wasn't perfect. "Sarvam" once came out as "sharvam". But the meaning always got through.

## Voice on both sides

Since I built it by speaking, it felt right that visitors could listen to it and talk to it too:

- **Know about me** walks you through my work, narrated in an Indian voice recorded with Sarvam AI.
- **Ask me anything** listens. Ask "What have you won?" out loud and it answers and scrolls you there.
- **Let's talk** sends your message straight to my inbox.

The answers are written from my real work, so it never makes things up.
